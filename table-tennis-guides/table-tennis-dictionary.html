Equipment & Gear Terms

Blade
The wooden paddle base before rubber is attached. Blade composition, number of plies, and stiffness determine the speed and control ceiling of a racket. Blades range from pure wood (control-oriented) to carbon or arylate-carbon composites (speed-oriented).
Rubber
The rubber sheet covering each side of the blade. Rubber choice is the single biggest factor in a player's spin and speed potential, more so than the blade itself.
Sponge
The layer between the blade and topsheet that adds speed and spin. Sponge thickness is measured in millimeters, typically ranging from 1.5mm to 2.2mm; thicker sponge generally means more speed and less control.
Topsheet
The outer rubber surface that contacts the ball. Its surface texture (smooth, pimpled, tacky, or grippy) determines how much friction it generates against the ball.
Inverted Rubber
Smooth rubber surface facing outward. The most common rubber type at all levels, allowing maximum spin generation and shot variety.
Pips-Out Rubber (Short Pips)
Rubber with the pimpled side facing outward. Reduces spin sensitivity and speeds up the game near the table, favored by blockers and hitters.
Long Pips
Extra-long pimpled rubber that reverses or disrupts incoming spin. A specialty rubber favored by defensive and disruptive players to create unpredictable returns.
Anti-Spin Rubber
Low-friction rubber designed to neutralize an opponent's spin almost entirely. Rare at high levels but effective for confusing less experienced opponents.
ITTF Approved
Equipment certified by the International Table Tennis Federation for tournament use, verified by a small stamp on the rubber.
Blade Speed Rating
Manufacturer classification (defense, all-round, offense, or offense+) indicating a blade's inherent speed potential.
Ply
A single layer of wood or composite material in a blade. Common builds include 5-ply all-wood, 7-ply all-wood, and carbon composite blades with 5 or 7 total layers.
Carbon Blade
A blade with carbon fiber layers woven between wood plies, adding speed, stiffness, and a larger sweet spot compared to all-wood blades.
Handle Types
Flared (FL), straight (ST), anatomic (AN), and Chinese penhold (CPEN) handle shapes, each affecting grip comfort and stroke mechanics differently.
Edge Tape
Protective tape applied to the blade's edges to prevent chipping from table or ball contact.
Booster/Speed Glue
Substances historically used to increase rubber elasticity and speed. Speed glue is now banned by the ITTF for health and fairness reasons; booster use is heavily restricted.
Racket Case
Protective storage case for the paddle, often with pockets for a spare rubber sheet or towel.
Rubber Sheet Weight
The mass of a rubber sheet, typically 30 to 50 grams, which affects overall racket balance and swing feel.
Tensor Rubber
A rubber technology using internal tension in the topsheet to boost speed and spin without external glue, now the industry standard.
Hardness Rating
A sponge's firmness, usually rated in degrees; harder sponges generally reward faster, more technical swings, while softer sponges are more forgiving.
Table Tennis Ball
The 40mm poly ball used in modern play, having replaced the older 38mm celluloid ball for safety and consistency reasons.
Ball Star Rating
A 1 to 3 star system indicating ball quality; 3-star balls are the only ones approved for ITTF competition.
Table Regulation Size
An official table measures 2.74m long, 1.525m wide, and 76cm high, with a net height of 15.25cm.
Playing Surface Finish
The matte coating on a regulation table that affects ball bounce consistency and visibility.
Net and Post Set
The regulation net assembly, 15.25cm high, that must be attached to the exact center of the table.
Robot (Training Robot)
An automated ball-feeding machine used for solo practice, allowing players to drill specific spin, speed, and placement patterns.
Multiball Training Equipment
A large basket or box of balls used by a coach to feed a player rapid, repeated shots for technical drilling.
Grip Tape/Overgrip
Material wrapped around a blade handle to improve sweat absorption and grip security.

Grip Styles

Shakehand Grip
The most common Western-style grip, holding the paddle as if shaking hands. Dominant in European, American, and increasingly Asian professional play.
Penhold Grip
A grip where the handle is held like a pen, with the blade extending downward. Historically dominant in Chinese and other East Asian playing styles.
Chinese Penhold
A penhold variation using primarily one side of the blade, with a wraparound backhand technique (RPB, or "reverse penhold backhand") now common at elite level to add a genuine backhand attack.
Japanese/Korean Penhold
A penhold variation that uses both sides of the blade like a shakehand grip, giving a more natural backhand than traditional Chinese penhold.
Seemiller Grip
A shakehand variation with the index finger running along the blade's edge, popularized by American player Danny Seemiller, often paired with an asymmetric rubber setup.
V-Grip
A rare shakehand variation where the racket is angled more vertically in the hand for extra wrist leverage on serves and flicks.
Deep Grip vs. Shallow Grip
How far up the handle a shakehand player holds the racket; deep grips favor power, shallow grips favor touch and control.
Thumb Position (Backhand Lock)
In shakehand grip, thumb placement on the blade affects backhand stability; a more locked thumb adds control on backhand blocks.
Grip Pressure
How tightly a player holds the paddle during a stroke; most coaches recommend a relaxed grip that tightens only at ball contact for maximum racket speed.
Finger Spread
How the index and middle fingers are positioned on the blade in shakehand grip, influencing forehand and backhand transition speed.

Strokes & Techniques

Forehand
A stroke played on the dominant hand side of the body, generally the more powerful of a player's two wings.
Backhand
A stroke played on the non-dominant hand side of the body, increasingly used as a primary attacking weapon in modern table tennis.
Topspin
A stroke that generates forward-rotating spin, causing the ball to dip sharply and accelerate off the bounce, the foundation of modern attacking play.
Backspin (Chop/Cut)
A stroke that generates backward-rotating spin, causing the ball to float, drop short, or die low off the bounce.
Sidespin
Spin causing the ball to curve in flight or skid sideways off the bounce, often combined with topspin or backspin on serves.
Loop
An aggressive topspin stroke and the primary attacking shot in modern table tennis, played with a low-to-high brushing motion.
Loop Drive
A faster, flatter loop that sacrifices some spin for extra pace, used to end points against a weaker return.
Counter-loop
Looping directly against an opponent's loop, an advanced topspin-on-topspin exchange requiring precise timing.
Drive
A flatter, faster stroke with less spin than a loop, used for quick, aggressive rallies close to the table.
Push
A short, controlled backspin shot used to keep the ball low and prevent an easy attack, most often played in response to a short serve.
Long Push
A deep backspin push aimed at an opponent's weaker side, harder to attack than a short push.
Flick (Flip)
An aggressive attacking stroke against a short ball, generated mostly with wrist and forearm snap rather than a full swing.
Banana Flick
A modern flick variation using heavy sidespin, popularized by top Chinese and European players for attacking short serves.
Chop
A defensive backspin stroke played well back from the table, the primary weapon of defensive-style players.
Chop-Block
A hybrid shot combining a short blocking motion with backspin, used to disrupt an opponent's rhythm close to the table.
Block
A short, compact stroke that redirects an opponent's attack using their own pace, requiring minimal backswing.
Punch Block
An active block with a small forward push added, generating extra pace rather than simply absorbing the incoming shot.
Smash
A powerful, flat attacking shot aimed to end the point outright, typically played against a high or slow ball.
Lob
A high defensive shot played from far off the table against a smash, buying time to reset court position.
Fish
A flatter defensive return played from mid-distance, carrying less spin than a chop and often used as a transition shot back to offense.
Drop Shot
A soft, short return that lands close to the net, catching an opponent out of position after they have moved away from the table.
Serve
The shot that starts a rally, which can carry topspin, backspin, sidespin, or a combination, and is often the most tactically important shot in a point.
Short Serve
A serve designed to bounce twice on the opponent's side if left untouched, limiting their ability to attack.
Long Serve (Deep Serve)
A fast serve aimed deep into the opponent's court, often used to catch a player expecting a short serve.
Pendulum Serve
A common forehand serve using a pendulum-like wrist motion, typically producing sidespin and backspin combinations.
Reverse Pendulum Serve
The mirror-image motion of a pendulum serve, producing the opposite sidespin direction.
Tomahawk Serve
A forehand serve struck with an upward chopping wrist motion, producing sidespin that curves away from a right-handed opponent's forehand.
Backhand Serve
A serve played from the backhand side, common in modern play for its deceptive spin variations.
Return of Serve
The shot made in response to a serve, considered one of the most technically demanding skills at high levels of play.
Half-Volley Block
A block taken very early after the bounce, close to the table, for extra speed and reduced reaction time for the opponent.
Sidespin Loop
A topspin loop with added sidespin, used to curve the ball wide of the table and pull an opponent off balance.
Flat Hit
A stroke with minimal spin and maximum pace, typically played against a high, slow ball.
Touch Shot
Any soft, controlled shot (drop shot, short push) relying on feel rather than power.
Footwork Stroke Combination
Practiced sequences (like forehand-forehand-backhand) that pair specific strokes with specific footwork patterns.

Spin Concepts

Spin Rate
The amount of rotation on the ball, directly affecting how it behaves off the paddle and table.
Heavy Spin
A shot with a high rate of rotation, difficult to read and control on the return.
No-Spin (Float/Knuckle Ball)
A serve or shot deliberately hit with minimal rotation to deceive an opponent expecting heavy spin.
Spin Reversal
The effect that occurs when long pips or antispin rubber contact a spinning ball, reversing or flattening its rotational effect on return.
Kill Spin
Neutralizing incoming spin with paddle angle and touch so it does not carry through to the return shot.
Spin Reading
The skill of visually judging an opponent's spin from their swing path and contact point, critical for serve returns.
Combination Spin
A serve or shot that mixes two spin types, such as topspin-sidespin or backspin-sidespin, to increase deception.
Spin Variation Serve
A serving strategy that uses an identical-looking motion to disguise different spin outcomes, forcing an opponent to guess.
Spin Decay
The gradual loss of spin on a ball as it travels through the air or after multiple bounces.
Racket Angle
The tilt of the paddle face at contact, the primary tool for both generating and neutralizing spin.

Tactics & Playing Styles

All-Round Player
A player skilled in both offense and defense with no single dominant style, adapting tactics to the opponent.
Attacker/Looper
A player whose game is built primarily around aggressive topspin attacks from both wings.
Defender/Chopper
A player who plays primarily from mid-to-far distance using backspin defense to wear down an opponent's patience and consistency.
Blocker (Penhold Blocker)
A player who relies on close-to-table blocking and quick reflexes rather than initiating heavy attacks.
Two-Winged Attack
A playing style attacking aggressively from both the forehand and backhand sides, standard at the elite level today.
Pips Hitter
A player using short pips rubber to play a fast, flat, close-to-table attacking style with reduced spin sensitivity.
Serve and Attack
A tactic focused on setting up an aggressive third-ball attack directly off a well-placed serve.
Third-Ball Attack
An aggressive shot played immediately after the serve and the opponent's return, considered the core modern attacking tactic.
Fifth-Ball Attack
The attacking opportunity that arises two shots later in a rally, common when a third-ball attack is neutralized.
Rally
A continuous exchange of shots between points, ranging from a few strokes to extended multi-shot exchanges.
Placement
Directing shots to specific table locations, such as the wide corners or the middle "elbow" zone, to control an opponent's positioning.
Middle/Elbow Play
Targeting the transition zone between an opponent's forehand and backhand, a classic tactic to force a weak or late shot.
Wide Angle Play
Using extreme cross-court or down-the-line placement to pull an opponent off the table and open the court.
Change of Pace
Mixing fast and slow shots to disrupt an opponent's timing and rhythm.
Change of Spin
Alternating heavy and light spin shots to keep an opponent guessing on contact and paddle angle.
Serve Variation Strategy
Systematically rotating serve types, spins, and placements throughout a match to prevent an opponent from adjusting.
Reading the Opponent
Anticipating an opponent's shot based on their stance, backswing, and racket angle before contact.
Footwork
Movement patterns used to position the body correctly for each stroke, considered as important as stroke technique itself.
Ready Position
The neutral, balanced stance a player returns to between shots, typically slightly crouched with weight on the balls of the feet.
Cross-Step
A footwork pattern used to cover wide shots, especially useful for reaching a wide forehand from a backhand-side start.
Side-Step (Shuffle Step)
Small lateral steps used for minor positional adjustments during close-to-table rallies.
Pivot
Rotating the body and stepping around to play a forehand shot from the backhand corner, a hallmark of aggressive forehand-dominant styles.
In-and-Out Movement
Footwork that shifts a player's distance from the table, moving in for an attack and back for defense.
Recovery Step
The footwork used to return to the ready position immediately after playing a shot, especially after being pulled wide.
Shadow Play (Shadow Practice)
Practicing strokes and footwork without a ball, used to build muscle memory.
Match Pacing
Deliberately controlling the speed of play, including between-point routines, to manage momentum and composure.
Momentum Shift
A noticeable change in a match's flow, often triggered by a string of points, that coaches and players actively try to manage.

Rules & Scoring

Let
A replayed point, most commonly when the serve clips the net cord and still lands legally in the opponent's court.
Edge Ball
A shot that lands exactly on the edge of the table, counted as a legal point even though it is difficult to read.
Expedite System
A rule speeding up long games; once triggered, the server must win the point within 13 total strokes or automatically lose it.
Game/Set
A game is won by the first player to reach 11 points with at least a 2-point lead; a match is typically the best of 5 or 7 games.
Deuce
A score of 10-10 (or higher tied scores), after which a player must win by a 2-point margin to take the game.
Fault
An illegal serve or other rule violation resulting in a point loss for the offending player.
Obstruction
Interference with the ball's natural path before it lands on the table, resulting in a point awarded to the opponent.
Legal Serve
A serve thrown at least 16cm vertically from an open, flat palm, struck behind the end line and visible to the opponent throughout.
Toss Height Rule
The specific requirement that the ball be tossed at least 16cm into the air before being struck on serve, designed to reduce serving deception.
Change of Ends
Switching sides of the table after each completed game, and also mid-way through the final deciding game once a player reaches 5 points.
Alternating Serves
The rule that service changes every 2 points during a game (or every point after 10-10), rotating between players.
Doubles Serve Rule
In doubles, serves must travel diagonally from the right-hand service court to the opponent's right-hand service court.
Doubles Rotation Rule
In doubles, partners must alternate hitting the ball on every shot throughout the rally.
Time-Out
A one-minute break a player or team may call once per match to consult with a coach.
Racket Inspection
The official checking of racket coverings for legality (color, ITTF approval, condition) before a match.
Two-Color Rule
The requirement that a racket's two rubber surfaces be different, easily distinguishable colors (commonly red and black).
Hidden Serve Rule
The rule banning a server's free arm or body from blocking the opponent's view of the ball toss and contact.
Net and Edge Judge
Officials who assist the umpire in close calls on net-cord serves and edge balls at higher-level events.
Point Penalty
A rule violation, such as illegal coaching timing or unsporting conduct, that results in a point or game being awarded to the opponent.
Warm-Up Period
The standard 2-minute practice period allowed immediately before a match begins.
Best of Format
The overall match structure, most commonly best-of-5 games in regular events and best-of-7 in finals or team competition.
Wheelchair Table Tennis Rules
A modified rule set, including different serve and table-edge regulations, used in Para table tennis competition.

Competition & Rating Terms

ITTF
International Table Tennis Federation, the sport's global governing body responsible for rules, rankings, and major events.
WTT (World Table Tennis)
The commercial competition arm of the ITTF, running the sport's major professional tour events including Star Contender and Champions events.
Ranking Points
Points awarded from tournament results, used to calculate a player's position in world and national rankings.
World Ranking
The ITTF's global player ranking list, updated regularly based on tournament results over a rolling period.
Seeding
Placement in a tournament bracket based on ranking, designed to avoid early-round matchups between top players.
Round Robin
A group stage format where every player in a group faces every other player before advancing to knockout rounds.
Knockout Stage (Single Elimination)
A tournament format where a loss results in immediate elimination from the event.
Singles/Doubles/Mixed Doubles
The three primary individual and pair event formats in competitive table tennis.
Team Event
A format where national or club teams compete, typically through a series of singles and doubles matches.
Umpire
The official who oversees a match, calls points, and enforces the rules at the table.
Referee
The senior official overseeing an entire competition venue, handling disputes beyond an umpire's authority.
Coach's Corner
The designated point during a timeout or between games where a coach may give brief tactical advice.
USATT Rating
The United States' national player rating system, used to seed domestic tournaments and track improvement.
TTCAN Rating
Table Tennis Canada's national rating system, used for seeding tournaments across Canadian clubs and events.
Elo-Based Rating System
A rating methodology (used by many national federations) that adjusts a player's number up or down based on match results against rated opponents.
Junior/Cadet/Hopes Categories
Age-based competitive divisions used in international junior table tennis events.
Para Table Tennis Classification
The classification system (classes 1-11) used to group Para athletes by disability type and functional ability for fair competition.
Olympic Table Tennis
Table tennis has been a full Olympic sport since the 1988 Seoul Games, featuring singles and team events.
World Table Tennis Championships
The sport's oldest and most prestigious event, held biennially (team and individual events alternate years).
ITTF World Tour/Grand Smash
Premier annual events within the WTT ecosystem carrying the highest ranking points and prize money.

Training & Coaching Terms

Multiball Drill
A high-repetition training method where a coach feeds balls rapidly from a basket, allowing far more repetitions than a normal rally.
Shadow Practice
Rehearsing strokes and footwork without a ball to build muscle memory and technique consistency.
Consistency Drill
A practice format focused on sustaining a rally pattern (such as forehand-to-forehand) for a set number of shots without error.
Random Drill
A practice format where shot placement or type varies unpredictably, training reaction and adaptability rather than fixed patterns.
Footwork Drill
A structured practice sequence pairing specific movement patterns (such as two-step side-to-side) with corresponding strokes.
Serve Practice
Dedicated solo practice time focused purely on serve spin, placement, and deception, often overlooked but critical for point control.
Video Analysis
Reviewing recorded match or practice footage to identify technical flaws or tactical patterns.
Periodization
A structured, phased approach to training that varies intensity and focus across a season to peak for key competitions.
Physical Conditioning
Off-table fitness training (footwork ladders, plyometrics, core work) supporting the explosive movement demands of table tennis.
Mental Game Training
Practice focused on focus, composure, and routine-building for competitive pressure situations.
Technical Correction
Coach-led adjustment of a flawed stroke mechanic, often broken down into isolated repetition before reintegration into play.
Tactical Debrief
A post-match review session analyzing what worked, what failed, and adjustments for future matches against the same or similar opponents.

Slang & Informal Terms

Chicken Wing
A slang term for an awkward, cramped backhand stroke caused by poor elbow positioning.
Junk Player
An informal term for a player using unconventional rubbers (long pips, antispin) to disrupt rhythm rather than out-hit opponents.
Hack
Informal slang for a weak, unorthodox, or scrappy shot that unexpectedly wins the point.
Table Tennis Elbow
A common overuse injury affecting the elbow tendons from repetitive stroke motion, informally named after the sport.
Ping-Pong Diplomacy
A historical term referring to the 1971 exchange of table tennis players between the United States and China that helped thaw Cold War relations.
Sandbagging
Informally entering a lower rating category than a player's true skill level to gain a competitive advantage.
Choking
Informal term for underperforming due to nerves or pressure in a critical moment of a match.
In the Zone
Informal term for a state of peak focus and performance during play.
