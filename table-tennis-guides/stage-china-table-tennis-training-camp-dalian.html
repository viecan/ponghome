Stage China: International Table Tennis Training Camp in Dalian

Fifteen days of structured, high volume table tennis training in a professional Chinese environment, built around individual correction and real matches against local players.

Pong Home Insights Hub · Training · 7 min read
PH
Pong Home Insights Hub · Coach-written training camp guide

Stage China is an international table tennis training camp held in Dalian, China, built for players who want to train the way top Chinese athletes train. Instead of a short clinic or a single seminar, participants spend around two weeks fully immersed in a professional training environment, working through a structured program, getting individual technical correction, and testing what they have learned in real matches against local players.

What Is Stage China

Stage China gives international players the opportunity to experience intensive table tennis training inside a professional Chinese training environment. The camp is held in Dalian, a city with a strong table tennis culture, and it is designed around one central idea: training volume alone does not create better players, so every session is built to help players find and fix the technical issues that are actually holding them back.

If you have wondered why so many serious players make the trip to train in China in the first place, our guide on why table tennis players travel to train in China covers the bigger picture. Stage China is one concrete way to put that experience into practice.

Camp Length and Daily Schedule

A standard Stage China camp runs for around 15 days. On table training takes up roughly 5 to 6 hours each day, split into a morning session and an afternoon session. That schedule gives players enough repetition to build new habits while still leaving room to recover between sessions, which matters over a camp that lasts more than two weeks.

  • Camp length: approximately 15 days.
  • Daily training time: around 5 to 6 hours on the table.
  • Session structure: a morning session and an afternoon session each day.

Price and What Is Included

The current price for Stage China is 160 dollars per person per day. That daily rate is all inclusive and covers everything a player needs for the camp:

  • Table tennis training: full access to the daily group and individual training program.
  • Accommodation: a place to stay for the full length of the camp.
  • Three meals per day: breakfast, lunch, and dinner included, so players can focus on training and recovery rather than logistics.

How the Training Program Works

Stage China follows a structured group training program, but it is not a one size fits all schedule. Coaches also build in individual technical correction and targeted training based on what each player specifically needs to improve. A junior working on backhand consistency and an adult player refining serve variation will not spend their camp doing identical drills.

The goal is not simply to add more hours on the table. The coaching staff wants players to identify the technical problems that matter most and make real, lasting improvements during their time in China, rather than leaving with tired arms and the same habits they arrived with.

Coaching note: The most valuable part of a camp like this is often not the extra reps, it is having an experienced eye watch your shot day after day and correct the same small flaw until it actually changes. That kind of consistent correction is hard to get in a short weekend clinic.

Challenge Matches: Testing What You Trained

Alongside the daily training program, Stage China organizes Challenge Matches, where participants compete against stronger local players. These matches are not just friendly hitting, they are a chance for players to test what they have worked on in training, adapt under pressure, and learn how to find solutions against opponents who are used to a faster, heavier style of play.

Playing regularly against stronger opposition is one of the fastest ways to expose gaps that do not show up in drilling. A serve that looks fine in practice, or a backhand that holds up against a training partner, gets a much harder test in a live Challenge Match.

Who Can Join: Youth and Adult Players

Stage China welcomes both youth and adult players. The camp has access to different levels of training partners, so players can be matched appropriately according to their level rather than being thrown into sessions that are too advanced or too easy. This makes the camp a realistic option for a wide range of players, from motivated juniors working toward higher level competition to adult players who simply want a serious, focused block of training.

The Stage China Experience Beyond the Table

Stage China is not only about spending hours at the table. Organizers also want international players to experience Chinese table tennis culture and daily life in China during their stay. For future camps, especially those aimed at younger players, the plan is to include more cultural and local experiences alongside the intensive training program, so the trip becomes more than just a training block on a calendar.

2027 Camp Dates

For 2027, camps are currently being planned for June, July, and August. Exact dates for each camp will be announced once they are confirmed, so players interested in joining should keep an eye on future updates rather than assuming a fixed start date.
