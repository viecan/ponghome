Fan Zhendong: Breaking Down the Game of a Champion

Inside the game of table tennis's reigning Olympic champion: how he attacks, how he wins the close ones, and what is on his blade right now.

Pong Home Team · Athlete · 8 min read
PH
Pong Home Team · Coach-written player profile

Few names carry as much weight in modern table tennis as Fan Zhendong. Since breaking into China's national team as a teenager, he has built a career defined by explosive attacking power, relentless consistency, and a rivalry with Ma Long and Wang Chuqin that has shaped an entire era of the sport. This is a look at how he plays, how he wins, what he has achieved, and what is currently on his racket.

Who Is Fan Zhendong

Fan Zhendong was born on January 22, 1997, in Guangzhou, China. He joined Guangdong's provincial team as a young child and moved up through China's junior system quickly, earning a spot on the national team's B squad at 14 and the full A team at just 15, becoming the youngest member of the Chinese national table tennis team at the time. His breakthrough came in his late teens, when he upset established stars in national and international events and began climbing the world rankings at a pace few players have matched.

He first reached the world number one ranking in April 2018 at age 21 and has spent more than a decade at or near the top of the sport, a level of sustained dominance that puts him among the greatest players table tennis has produced.

Playing Style: The Explosive Two Wing Attacker

Fan plays a right handed shakehand grip and is best known for a powerful, all court attacking style built around two wings that are both genuine weapons. Where many players lean heavily on one side, Fan can open, counter, and finish points equally well off his forehand and backhand.

  • Explosive footwork: His movement around the table lets him get his body behind the ball on almost every shot, which is what allows his loops to carry so much weight even at high speed.
  • Heavy first ball topspin: Fan looks to attack the very first opportunity in a rally rather than waiting for a setup ball, putting opponents on the back foot immediately.
  • Powerful backhand loop: While his forehand is his primary weapon, his backhand loop is strong enough to attack from deep in the court, which removes an escape route many opponents rely on against forehand dominant players.
  • Physical strength: A sturdy, powerful build lets him generate unusual power on both wings without sacrificing the fast recovery needed for the next shot.

Tactics: How Fan Zhendong Wins Points

Fan's tactical game is built around taking control early and never giving it back. A few patterns show up again and again in his matches:

  • Serve and attack the third ball: He uses short, low serves to limit his opponent's options, then looks to loop the return hard before the opponent can set their own rhythm.
  • Mid distance counterlooping: When rallies extend, Fan often steps back slightly and out-loops opponents from mid distance, trading power for power and usually winning that exchange.
  • Changing pace and placement: Against players who read his power well, he will mix in slower, heavier spin balls or attack wide angles to break their timing rather than simply hitting harder.
  • Mental composure at deuce: Fan is known for staying calm in tight games, a trait that has repeatedly shown up in deciding sets of major finals against rivals like Ma Long and Wang Chuqin.
Coaching note: Club players can borrow the core idea even without Fan's power: attack the first loose ball you get in a rally instead of waiting for a perfect one. Passivity gives good opponents time to reset.

Career Timeline and Major Achievements

Fan Zhendong's résumé is one of the deepest in the sport's history. Career highlights include:

  • 2012: Became the youngest member of the Chinese national table tennis team at age 15.
  • 2018: Reached the world number one ranking for the first time at age 21.
  • 2023: Won the World Table Tennis Championships men's singles title, cementing his status as the world's top player.
  • 2024: Won Olympic gold in men's singles at the Paris Olympics, completing a career Grand Slam by holding the Olympic, World Championship, and World Cup singles titles.
  • Multiple Olympic and World Championship team gold medals alongside China's national squad.
  • Four World Cup singles titles across his career, along with numerous ITTF World Tour and Champions League titles at club level.

His combination of individual and team success across more than a decade makes him one of the most complete champions the sport has ever seen, and his career Grand Slam places him alongside a very short list of players in table tennis history.

Why People Love Fan Zhendong

Fan's popularity goes beyond his results. Fans and commentators consistently point to a few qualities:

  • Humility in defeat: After losing the Tokyo 2020 singles final to Ma Long, Fan was widely praised for his gracious response and public respect for his teammate and rival.
  • A relatable nickname: Fans affectionately call him "Little Fatty," a nickname that reflects genuine warmth toward him rather than his physique, and it has become part of his public identity.
  • A career defined by rivalry: His long running competition with Ma Long, and more recently with Wang Chuqin and the Lebrun brothers, has produced some of the most watched matches in modern table tennis.
  • Consistency over the long term: Staying near the top of the world rankings for over a decade, through multiple generations of challengers, has earned him respect even from players who regularly beat him early in their careers.

Equipment: What Is On Fan Zhendong's Racket

Fan Zhendong plays with the Butterfly Fan Zhendong ALC, his own signature blade released by Butterfly after he signed with the brand in November 2021. It is built on the same platform as the well known Viscaria and Timo Boll ALC blades, with a slightly thicker core layer that adds direct power while keeping enough dwell time for his heavy spin game.

His rubber setup has become one of the most closely watched storylines in professional table tennis equipment. For most of his career, Fan's forehand weapon has been DHS Hurricane 3 National with a blue sponge, a hard, tacky Chinese rubber that has powered the forehand loops of generations of Chinese national team players. During the 2025 to 2026 season he experimented with Butterfly Zyre 03 on his forehand, a hybrid Japanese rubber with more built in spring and a different throw angle. The switch drew attention after a run of tough losses to the Lebrun brothers, since a small timing difference from an unfamiliar rubber can matter enormously at the top level of the sport.

Fan has since been moving back toward Hurricane 3 for major events, and reports from recent tournaments describe him choosing his forehand rubber based on the table and ball brand in play, leaning on Hurricane 3 National against DHS and Nittaku equipment while occasionally using Butterfly rubber on both wings against other brands. On his backhand, Fan uses Butterfly Dignics 09C, one of the brand's top tier rubbers, which pairs well with his powerful two wing attacking style.
