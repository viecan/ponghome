Troubleshooting

10 Common Table Tennis Problems and How to Fix Them

The recurring mistakes that show up at almost every level of play, diagnosed, and paired with a practical fix you can try in your next session.

Pong Home Team · Troubleshooting · 9 min read
PH
Pong Home Team · Coach-written troubleshooting guide

Most table tennis frustrations aren't random, they're patterns. The same handful of technical and tactical issues show up again and again across players of very different skill levels, from beginners to club regulars. Below are 10 of the most common ones we hear about, with a quick diagnosis of what's usually causing each and a specific adjustment to try.

Technique

Why Does My Backhand Keep Going Into the Net?

Likely cause

A racket angle that's too closed, or not enough upward brush, especially against backspin. If your elbow drops during the stroke, the racket face naturally closes further, making the net miss even more likely.

Try this

Open the racket face slightly more than feels natural, and focus on brushing up and through the ball rather than pushing it forward flat. Keep your elbow at a consistent height through the stroke instead of letting it drop.

Technique

Why Does My Forehand Keep Going Long?

Likely cause

An open racket angle paired with too much forward drive and not enough topspin to bring the ball down. This is common when players prioritize power before they've built consistent spin.

Try this

Close the racket face slightly, and think "brush up and over" rather than "hit through." Make sure you're finishing the stroke fully rather than stopping short, since an incomplete stroke tends to produce flat, uncontrolled power instead of topspin.

Technique

Why Can't I Return Heavy Backspin?

Likely cause

Most players either don't open the racket angle enough, or try to drive through backspin the same way they'd hit a flat or topspin ball, both send the return into the net.

Try this

Open the racket face more than feels natural, and use a longer, lifting brush that travels from low to high. Slow the stroke down slightly so the rubber's grip does the lifting work, rather than relying on raw arm speed to power through the backspin.

Technique

Why Can't I Generate Spin?

Likely cause

Spin comes from a thin, grazing, tangential contact on the ball, not from hitting it harder. A full-arm swing without wrist snap tends to produce speed without much spin.

Try this

Focus on a fast wrist and forearm snap with a thin, brushing contact rather than swinging your whole arm through the ball. Also check your rubber's condition, worn or aged rubber loses grip and makes generating spin significantly harder no matter how good your technique is.

Focus

Why Do I Keep Missing Easy Balls?

Likely cause

Ironically, "easy" balls are often missed because players relax their focus and footwork on shots that feel low-risk, leading to a rushed or off-balance setup.

Try this

Treat every ball, including obviously easy ones, with the same setup discipline: move your feet into position first, every time, rather than reaching with just your arm. Resist the temptation to go for unnecessary extra power on a shot you assume you'll win regardless.

Mental Game

Why Do I Lose Points at 9-9 or in Close Games?

Likely cause

Close scores often cause players to abandon their normal shot selection out of nervousness, either pushing too safely or going for a risky winner that isn't part of their usual game.

Try this

Stick to the shots and patterns that got you to 9-9 in the first place. The fix here is mostly mental: resist the instinct to change your entire approach under pressure, and trust the game plan that was already working.

Technique

Why Do I Keep Popping Up Short Serves?

Likely cause

This typically happens from using too much arm on a shot that needs a short, controlled touch, or from making contact too late as the ball starts to drop.

Try this

Get low and step in close to the ball rather than reaching from a standing position. Use a short, wrist-driven stroke instead of a full swing, and aim to make contact at the top of the bounce rather than waiting, late contact naturally forces an upward, popped-up return.

Technique

Why Does My Loop Go Into the Net?

Likely cause

Almost always caused by not brushing upward enough relative to how hard you're driving forward. This is especially common when looping against backspin, where extra lift is needed just to clear the net.

Try this

Increase the vertical, upward component of your brush rather than the forward drive. Drop your contact point slightly lower on the ball to generate more lift, and check that your racket isn't closing too early in the stroke.

Footwork

Why Am I Always Late to the Ball?

Likely cause

Being consistently late is usually a footwork problem, not a hand-speed problem, specifically, reaching with the arm instead of moving the feet first.

Try this

Focus on a quick first step and recovering to a ready stance in between shots, rather than staying flat-footed until the last moment. Also try watching the opponent's racket at the moment of contact rather than the ball itself, since that's where the earliest cues about direction and speed actually come from.

Tactics

Why Do I Keep Losing to Defensive Players?

Likely cause

Defensive players often win by inducing impatience, many opponents try to end the point too early with one big shot and hand over easy errors instead of building the point.

Try this

Focus on constructing the point with consistent, well-placed spin rather than looking for a single finishing shot. Vary your placement and depth to disrupt their rhythm, and go in expecting longer rallies than you're used to, patience is usually the deciding factor against strong defense.

Fixing These On Your Own vs. With a Coach

A lot of these issues share a root cause: racket angle, contact point, and footwork timing, which is part of why fixing one often improves several of the others at once. That said, these are also exactly the kind of small technical details that are hard to self-diagnose accurately, since it's difficult to see your own racket angle or contact point in real time. If you've been stuck on one of these patterns for a while despite practicing, a session or two with a coach who can watch and correct in person is often the fastest way through it.
