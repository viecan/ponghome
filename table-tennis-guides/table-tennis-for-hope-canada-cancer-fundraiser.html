Table Tennis for Hope: Canada's Ping Pong Fundraiser for Cancer Research

How a grassroots table tennis initiative is turning tournaments in New Brunswick and Newfoundland into real money for the Canadian Cancer Society, with a goal of going coast to coast.

Pong Home Insights Hub · Club Culture · 6 min read
PH
Pong Home Insights Hub · Community and fundraising feature

Nearly one in two Canadians is expected to be diagnosed with cancer in their lifetime. A grassroots initiative called Table Tennis for Hope is betting that a ping pong table can be part of the response, one tournament at a time.

Table Tennis for Hope is a Canadian fundraising initiative that combines table tennis with cancer fundraising. Instead of a typical charity run or gala, the organization builds its fundraising around table tennis tournaments held in communities across the country, giving players of all ages and skill levels a reason to compete, meet other members of their local table tennis scene, and contribute to something bigger than the scoreboard.

Every dollar raised through a Table Tennis for Hope event goes to the Canadian Cancer Society (CCS). The organization states plainly that 100 percent of funds raised go to CCS, supporting cancer research, patient support services, and other programs the society runs across the country.

The Idea Behind It: Table Tennis as a Platform, Not Just a Sport

Table Tennis for Hope was built around a simple premise: table tennis can be a platform for something bigger than competition. A tournament already brings people together around a shared love of the game. Table Tennis for Hope layers a second purpose on top of that, turning entry fees, donations, and community sponsorships into direct support for cancer research and care.

For players, that means a tournament weekend does double duty. You get real competition across skill divisions, a chance to connect with your local table tennis community, and the knowledge that your entry fee is doing something beyond deciding a winner.

From Moncton to Paradise: The Tournaments So Far

Table Tennis for Hope has already run two tournaments on opposite sides of Atlantic Canada, and both point to the same pattern: strong local turnout, multiple skill divisions, and a genuine fundraising result for CCS.

  • Moncton, New Brunswick, the first major Table Tennis for Hope tournament, raised approximately $1,200 for the Canadian Cancer Society.
  • Paradise, Newfoundland and Labrador, held on August 15, 2026, at the Rotary Paradise Youth and Community Centre, brought together 40+ participants and raised $1,507 for the Canadian Cancer Society.

Together, the two events have raised roughly $2,700 for cancer research and support services, a meaningful total for an initiative still in its first year of tournaments.

The Newfoundland event also showed how the format scales for mixed-skill communities. It featured A, B, C, and D divisions, so newer recreational players and experienced competitive players each had a bracket suited to their level, along with a 3-point doubles event that kept the day social and welcoming for players who came with a friend or partner rather than to grind through singles matches all day.

A Community Effort, On and Off the Table

The Newfoundland tournament is also a good example of how much of this kind of fundraising happens off the table. The event drew support from local businesses, volunteers, and members of the Newfoundland table tennis community well before the first serve.

  • Rocket Bakery donated more than 100 granola bars for participants and volunteers.
  • The Rooms donated 2 tickets as part of the event's prizing and community support.
  • Chatters donated over $1,000 worth of products and gift cards.

None of that shows up on a scoreboard, but it is a large part of why the tournament worked. Local businesses covered snacks and prizing, volunteers ran the floor, and the club community that already exists around table tennis in the region showed up to make the day happen. It is a template that other clubs and cities could realistically copy.

The Long-Term Vision: A Coast-to-Coast-to-Coast Network

Table Tennis for Hope has been explicit that Moncton and Paradise are the starting point, not the ceiling. The long-term goal is to expand into additional provinces across Canada, eventually building a nationwide network of Table Tennis for Hope tournaments connected through the sport. Toronto and Vancouver are two of the next cities on that map, and getting a tournament off the ground in either city will depend on local table tennis clubs stepping forward to host, organize, and rally their own communities behind it.

Beyond the fundraising totals, the initiative is also framed as a way to grow table tennis itself, by creating opportunities for players, clubs, volunteers, and businesses to get involved in something with a clear, shared purpose. A club that hosts a Table Tennis for Hope event is not just running a tournament, it is introducing new players to the sport, giving its regulars a reason to show up and compete, and putting the club's name in front of the local business community as a partner worth supporting.

How to Get Involved

Table Tennis for Hope is still a young initiative, which means there is room for individual players, clubs, and sponsors to have a real impact on where it goes next.

  • Players can register for an upcoming tournament and compete in a division that fits their level.
  • Clubs and organizers looking to host a similar event locally can use a free tool like Pong Home's Arena bracket generator to run divisions and doubles brackets on the day.
  • Local businesses can offer prizes, gift cards, or sponsorship, following the example set by Rocket Bakery, The Rooms, and Chatters in Newfoundland.
  • Volunteers can help with check-in, scorekeeping, and logistics, the kind of behind-the-scenes work that made the Paradise tournament run smoothly.

The official Table Tennis for Hope website lists upcoming events and a take action page for anyone who wants to register, volunteer, or become a partner. The organization can also be reached at tabletennisforhope@gmail.com or on Instagram at @table.tennis.for.hope.

Quick Answers

How much has Table Tennis for Hope raised so far?

Roughly $2,700 combined across its first two tournaments: about $1,200 in Moncton, New Brunswick, and $1,507 in Paradise, Newfoundland and Labrador, with 100 percent of funds going to the Canadian Cancer Society.

Is Table Tennis for Hope only for competitive players?

No. The A through D division structure and the 3-point doubles event are designed specifically so recreational and beginner players can take part alongside more experienced competitors.

Where is Table Tennis for Hope headed next?

The organization's long-term goal is to expand beyond New Brunswick and Newfoundland and Labrador into additional provinces, building a coast-to-coast-to-coast network of tournaments connected through table tennis. Toronto and Vancouver are among the cities on that expansion path, and Table Tennis for Hope will need local clubs in both cities to step up as hosts, volunteers, and community partners to help bring a tournament to their area.

Cancer touches nearly every family in Canada in some way. Table Tennis for Hope is a reminder that a sport a lot of us already play for fun can also be turned into something that funds research and supports the people going through treatment right now. That is a rally worth getting behind. 🏓❤️
