Miwa Harimoto: Japan's Next Great Hope Rises to World Number 3

How the younger sister of Tomokazu Harimoto built her own fearless, attacking game and climbed to world number 3, becoming Japan's newest source of national pride.

Pong Home Insights Hub · Athlete · 8 min read
PH
Pong Home Insights Hub · Coach-written player profile

Japan has waited a long time for a homegrown women's star capable of standing toe to toe with China's best, and in Miwa Harimoto, the country may finally have found her. Younger sister of world number two Tomokazu Harimoto, Miwa has spent the past few years climbing the world rankings with a fearless, attacking game of her own, and she now sits inside the top 3 in the world, the highest position any Japanese woman has reached in years. This is her story, her game, and why an entire nation is rallying behind her.

Who Is Miwa Harimoto

Miwa Harimoto was born on June 16, 2008, in Sendai, Miyagi Prefecture, Japan, into a table tennis family. Her parents were Chinese born players and coaches who settled in Japan, and in the spring of 2014 the family became naturalized Japanese citizens, taking the surname Harimoto alongside her older brother Tomokazu. She started playing table tennis at just two years old, following the same path that made her brother a household name, and it did not take long for her own talent to stand out.

Miwa announced herself on the international stage early. At the 2018 China Junior Open she won the U13 title at just 10 years old, and a strong run to the U18 best eight at the Taipei Golden Junior Open the same year put the Japanese table tennis world on notice. By 2021, she had gone undefeated on the way to sweeping the U15 singles, women's doubles, mixed doubles, and team titles at the ITTF World Youth Championships, a statement result that confirmed she was not just her brother's sister, but a serious talent of her own.

Playing Style: Fast, Fearless, and Built to Attack

Like her brother, Miwa plays a right handed shakehand grip built around speed and aggression rather than patient rallying. She looks to take control of the point early and rarely gives an opponent time to settle into a comfortable rhythm.

  • Early ball contact: Miwa takes the ball close to the table and on the rise, a Harimoto family trademark that compresses her opponent's reaction time on both wings.
  • Powerful backhand: Her backhand drive is a genuine weapon, capable of ending points outright or forcing weak returns that she can finish with the forehand.
  • Fearless shot selection: Even against far more experienced Chinese opponents, she consistently goes for aggressive, high risk shots rather than playing it safe, a mentality that has been central to her rapid rise.
  • Composure under pressure: Despite her age, she has repeatedly shown the nerve to play her natural attacking game in tight, high stakes moments against elite competition.
Coaching note: Club players working on their own backhand can learn a lot from how Miwa commits fully to the shot rather than steering it safely back into play. Full commitment, even on lower percentage shots, is often what separates a promising attacker from a dangerous one.

Career Timeline and Major Achievements

Miwa's rise has been fast even by the standards of a family known for producing prodigies. Key milestones include:

  • 2018: Won the U13 singles title at the China Junior Open at age 10, an early signal of her talent on the international junior circuit.
  • 2021: Went undefeated to win the U15 singles, women's doubles, mixed doubles, and team titles at the ITTF World Youth Championships.
  • 2022 Asian Games: Helped Japan win a bronze medal in the women's team event in Hangzhou, and alongside partner Miyuu Kihara, upset China's Wang Manyu and Sun Yingsha pairing in the quarterfinal, a genuine shock result against two of the sport's biggest names.
  • 2024: Selected as the third women's team member for Japan at the Paris Olympics, where the Japanese women's team won a silver medal, a result Miwa contributed to as part of the national squad.
  • 2026: Reached a career high world ranking of number 3 in May, cementing her status as one of the leading women's players on the planet and Japan's top ranked woman.

Why Japan Is Rallying Behind Her

There is a genuine sense of national pride building around Miwa Harimoto, and it is easy to see why. A few things stand out about her growing following:

  • A sibling story fans can follow together: With Tomokazu already established as one of the top men in the world, watching Miwa climb the women's rankings gives Japanese fans two Harimotos to cheer for at the same major events.
  • A real challenger to Chinese dominance: China has controlled the top of the women's game for years, and every big win Miwa picks up against a Chinese opponent carries extra weight for a country hungry to see that grip loosened.
  • Home country pride at the Olympics: Being part of Japan's silver medal winning women's team at the Paris Olympics gave the whole country a shared moment to celebrate, with Miwa as one of the players who helped get the team there.
  • Still just getting started: Reaching world number 3 while still a teenager suggests her ceiling may be even higher, and Japanese fans know they could be watching the early chapters of something special.

Equipment: What Is On Harimoto's Racket

Miwa Harimoto is a longtime Butterfly sponsored athlete, and her setup is built for the same fast, attacking style that defines her game. She plays with the Butterfly Harimoto Tomokazu Innerforce Super ALC blade, the same signature blade line associated with her brother, chosen for its balance of speed and control at the close to the table style both siblings favor.

On both forehand and backhand she plays Butterfly Zyre 03, a hybrid Japanese rubber that combines strong built in spring with dependable control, well suited to her early contact, high tempo game. It is a setup that mirrors the direction much of the Japanese national team has moved in recent years as players search for more raw speed without sacrificing consistency.
