Every Table Tennis Player's Excuses After Losing

Dead rubber, bad lighting, an old injury that's suddenly acting up again. 10 excuses every table tennis player has heard, or used, right after a loss.

Pong Home Insights Hub · Club Culture · 6 min read
PH
Pong Home Insights Hub · Coach-written club culture piece

Every table tennis club has a soundtrack, and it isn't just the sound of the ball. It's the low murmur of excuses drifting off the table right after a loss. Some are half true. Some are pure theater. All of them are extremely familiar to anyone who's spent real time around the sport. Here are 10 of the most reliable ones, and what's usually actually going on underneath each.

1
Equipment

"My rubber is dead"

Sometimes true, often exaggerated. Rubber does lose grip over time, but it rarely dies mid-match in the exact 15 minutes it takes to lose a close third game. Worth checking honestly, since the fix is genuinely simple if it's real.

2
Preparation

"I didn't warm up properly"

Also known as the classic pre-loss insurance policy, deployed either before the match as a disclaimer or after as an explanation. Either way, it's rarely mentioned after a win.

3
Environment

"The lighting here is different"

A surprisingly common one, and to be fair, lighting genuinely does affect how well you read the ball at higher levels. That said, it's suspicious how the lighting only ever seems to become a problem after the score goes the wrong way.

4
Physical

"I'm still nursing an old injury"

Often technically true, rarely relevant to the specific point that was just lost. The injury has usually been "acting up" for roughly the same amount of time as the losing streak.

5
Equipment

"The ball felt off"

An oddly specific complaint given that both players were using the exact same ball for the exact same rally. Still gets deployed with total conviction almost every time.

6
Focus

"I was distracted"

Usually blamed on a noisy neighboring table, a phone buzzing somewhere in the building, or someone walking past at the exact wrong moment. Focus is a real factor in table tennis, but it's rarely this precisely timed.

7
Sportsmanship

"I let you win the first game to be nice"

A bold retroactive strategy reveal, always announced only after the match is fully lost. No one has ever claimed to be "letting" their opponent win while the match was still in progress.

8
Format

"I don't usually play this format"

Deployed after a loss in whatever format was just played, regardless of what that format was. Somehow every format is the unfamiliar one when it matters.

9
Technical

"My grip felt weird today"

A genuinely useful diagnosis when it's true, and a wonderfully vague one when it's not, since it's nearly impossible for anyone else to verify.

10
Practice

"I'm just rusty"

The evergreen classic. Works regardless of how recently the person actually played, and somehow still gets used by players who were at the club three days ago.

Why We All Do This

None of this is really about dishonesty. Table tennis is a sport with almost no equipment or environment to hide behind, which means a loss feels unusually personal compared to team sports where blame can be spread around. An excuse, even a slightly ridiculous one, softens that for a moment. The healthier long-term move is turning that same instinct into something useful: instead of explaining away a loss, use it to actually identify what needs work. If you keep hearing yourself reach for the same excuse over and over, that's usually a pretty reliable signal of where your real gap is.
