Doubles Play Explained

Doubles Table Tennis Rules: Rotation, Serving, and Positioning Explained

Everything you need to know about serving order, rotation, and positioning before your next doubles match.

Pong Home Team · Doubles · 5 min read
PH
Pong Home Team · Coach-written doubles rules guide

Doubles table tennis rewards quick footwork and even quicker communication, but most new doubles players lose points not to their opponents' shots, but to their own confusion about rotation and serving order. Here is exactly how the rules work.

Serving Order

How does the serving order work in table tennis doubles?

Each pair chooses a serving order at the start of the match. Player A of the serving team serves to Player A of the receiving team. After two points, the serve rotates to Player A of the receiving team, who now serves to Player B of the original serving team, continuing the rotation every two points in this cycle: A serves to A, then A serves to B, then B serves to B, then B serves to A, and back to the start.

Serve Placement

Does the diagonal serve rule apply in doubles?

Yes. Every serve in doubles must land in the server's right half of the table and then bounce in the receiver's right half of the table, based on each player's position at the moment of serving. This is different from singles, where the ball can be served to any part of the opponent's side.

Alternating Shots

Do partners have to alternate hitting the ball during a doubles rally?

Yes. After the serve and return, each player on a team must alternate strokes. A team cannot have the same player hit the ball twice in a row during a rally. This rule is what makes doubles footwork and positioning so different from singles, since both players are constantly moving in and out of hitting position.

Common Mistakes

What happens if a doubles team hits the ball out of order?

Hitting out of order results in a point loss for the team that made the error. This is one of the most common doubles mistakes for players used to singles play, especially in fast exchanges near the table where instinct can override the rotation pattern.

Between Games

How does the serving order change between games in a doubles match?

At the start of each new game, the receiving pair from the previous game becomes the serving pair, and the two players on that team can choose to swap their serving order. The receiving order for the opposing team is also determined by who received first in the previous game, following the same rotation pattern.

Positioning Tips for New Doubles Teams

Beyond the formal rules, good doubles play comes down to clearing out of your partner's way immediately after your shot. A simple habit that helps: after you hit, take one step to the side and slightly back, opening a direct path to the ball for your partner. Pairs who move in a smooth clockwise or counterclockwise rotation around the table almost always outplay pairs who stand still and get in each other's way.

Coaching tip: Practice the serving rotation out loud before a match. Saying "I serve, you receive, then serve" a few times prevents the confusion that costs easy points early in close games.

Doubles Rotation at a Glance

Point SequenceServerReceiver
Points 1 to 2Team 1, Player ATeam 2, Player A
Points 3 to 4Team 2, Player ATeam 1, Player B
Points 5 to 6Team 1, Player BTeam 2, Player B
Points 7 to 8Team 2, Player BTeam 1, Player A

The cycle repeats from point 9 onward until the game ends, then resets with a new serving order at the start of the next game.
