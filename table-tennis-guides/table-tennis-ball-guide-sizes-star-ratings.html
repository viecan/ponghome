Table Tennis Ball Guide: 40mm Sizes, Star Ratings, and Materials Explained

What ball size, star ratings, and materials actually mean, and which balls are worth buying for your practice.

Pong Home Team · Equipment · 5 min read
PH
Pong Home Team · Coach-written equipment guide

A ball seems like the simplest piece of equipment on the table, but size, material, and star rating all meaningfully affect how it plays. Here is what those numbers and letters actually mean.

Ball Size: Why 40mm Became Standard

Table tennis balls were historically 38mm in diameter until the sport's governing body increased the standard to 40mm in the early 2000s. The larger ball moves slightly slower and offers marginally more visible spin, changes intended to make professional matches more watchable for spectators and broadcast audiences. Every ball sold for competitive play today uses the 40mm standard.

Understanding Star Ratings

RatingTypical UseQuality Level
1 starCasual, recreational playLower roundness and durability tolerance
2 starClub-level practiceMid-range consistency
3 starOfficial competitionStrictest roundness, bounce, and material standards

For serious practice or any competitive play, three-star balls are worth the extra cost. Their consistency directly affects how reliably you can read spin and bounce, which matters far more once your technique reaches an intermediate level or beyond.

Poly vs Celluloid Balls

Table tennis balls transitioned from celluloid, a flammable material that posed shipping and safety concerns, to poly plastic balls starting in the mid 2010s. Poly balls are slightly larger in practice, bounce a little differently, and require marginally more effort to generate the same spin as the older celluloid balls, a change that affected professional technique and equipment choices industry-wide.

Coaching tip: If you are buying balls in bulk for club or home practice, one or two star balls are perfectly fine for basic drilling. Save three-star balls for match play and serious spin or placement practice, since their higher cost adds up quickly for high-volume repetition drills.

Color: White or Orange

Regulation balls come in white or orange, and the choice is based purely on table and background color contrast rather than performance. Tournaments typically use whichever color offers the best visibility against the table color and playing environment.

Frequently Asked Questions

Ball Basics

What does the star rating on a table tennis ball mean?

Star ratings, typically from one to three stars, indicate ball quality and roundness consistency. Three-star balls meet the strictest manufacturing tolerances and are the only balls approved for official ITTF competition.

Ball Basics

Are all table tennis balls the same size?

Modern regulation balls are all 40mm in diameter, a size standardized in the early 2000s to slightly slow down the professional game. Older 38mm balls are no longer used in competitive play.
